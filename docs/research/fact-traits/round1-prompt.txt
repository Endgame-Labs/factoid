You are helping design Factoid, a Python framework that turns documents into facts linked to entities. Answer in plain markdown, under about 150 lines. Be concrete. No preamble.

How Factoid works:
- A fact is one self-contained sentence, plus exact source quotes (who said it, when), linked to one or more entities.
- Labels are yes/no tags a project defines per domain.
- NEW: traits. When labeling a fact, a fast decision model (one pass, returns a probability per option, no generated text) also judges the fact's inherent content against fixed rubrics that ship with Factoid. They must work across any corpus a user might bring.

The draft traits were designed with a sales CRM corpus in mind (calls, emails, deal records):
1. Kind of fact (choice): stable (true for years: "Northwind is headquartered in Austin"), slow-changing (role, plan, relationship: "Dana is VP of Sales"), current state (status/number that moves in days or weeks: "The renewal is in Negotiation"), event (happened at a time, stays true as history: "Northwind signed the pilot on March 3"), plan (intended or expected; expires when its date passes: "They plan to expand to Europe in Q3"), decision (a choice someone made: "Northwind chose annual billing over monthly").
2. Validity (choice over ordered ranges): a day, a week, a month, a quarter, a year, several years, permanent. Factoid stores valid_until = when stated + the median range.
3. Optional: hedged (yes/no), and opinion vs observed (choice).

Traits are used to: rank stale facts lower in search; filter to facts likely still true at a date; decide whether two disagreeing facts are a supersede (state changed over time) or a real contradiction; let a contradiction go stale when its facts expire; list facts due for re-checking; and calibrate validity predictions against how long facts actually last.

Your task:
1. Brainstorm your own list of corpus types people would plausibly feed a system like this. Make them as different from CRM and from each other as you can. Pick the 6 that stress the draft traits hardest, and say briefly why you picked them.
2. For each: 3 example facts, how the draft traits classify them, and where the traits fail, mislead, or are missing something.
3. Gaps that show up across several corpora. For each, say whether it needs a new trait, a new option on an existing trait, or is better left to labels or entities.
4. Propose a final general trait set: each trait, its type (yes/no, choice, or ordered ranges), its options with a one-line rubric each, and why it earns its place. Keep it small: each trait costs a question on every fact. Mark which are on by default.
5. Name any risk in using a model's guess of validity at all, and how to keep it honest.
